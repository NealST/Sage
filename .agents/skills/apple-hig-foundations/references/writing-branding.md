## Writing
The words you choose within your app are an essential part of its user experience.

### Best practices
**Getting started**
- Determine your app's voice: know your audience and the vocabulary they find familiar; decide how you want people to feel (a banking app conveys trust and stability, a game excitement and fun); keep a list of common terms for consistency.
- Match your tone to the context: vary tone with the situation (a workout goal vs. a payment error); situational factors affect what you say and how you display it.
- Be clear: choose easily understood words, check that each word earns its place, use fewer words when possible, read your writing out loud when in doubt.
- Write for everyone: simple, plain language with accessibility and localization in mind; avoid jargon and gendered terminology.

**In the interface**
- Consider each screen's purpose: order elements with the most important information first; format text for easy reading; split multiple ideas across screens and think about the flow between them.
- Be action oriented: use active voice and clear labels; label buttons and links with verbs ("Send" beats "Let's do it!"); use descriptive link text instead of "Click here" (critical for screen reader users).
- Build language patterns: consistency builds familiarity and makes writing easier — reuse them.
- Adopt capitalization rules aligned with your app's style and apply consistently (title case = formal, sentence case = casual; e.g., title case for all alerts, sentence case for all headlines).
- Use consistent language across multi-step flows: signal the start ("Get Started"), hint at the next step ("Continue"/"Next"), and signal completion ("Done").
- Use possessive pronouns sparingly ("Favorites" = "Your Favorites"); if used, be consistent and don't switch perspectives; avoid "we" entirely — "Unable to load content" is clearer than "We're having trouble loading this content."
- Write for how people use each device: consistent language across devices, but describe gestures correctly ("tap" on iPhone/iPad, not "click"); small screens (iPhone, Apple Watch) require brevity; TV screens are shared and viewed from a distance — consider who's addressed and keep text brief.
- Provide clear next steps on blank screens: empty states can welcome and educate, but must be useful, contextual, and guide people to an action (with a button or link); don't show crucial information in temporary empty states.
- Write clear error messages: prevent errors when possible; display messages close to the problem; avoid blame; state what to do ("Choose a password with at least 8 characters", not "That password is too short"); avoid interjections like "oops!"; if language can't fix a recurring error, rethink the interaction.
- Choose the right delivery method: weigh urgency, importance, context, and supporting information needs; use an appropriate tone (notifications, alerts, action sheets).
- Keep settings labels clear and simple: describe what the setting does when on (people infer the opposite); link directly to a setting rather than describing where it is.
- Show hints in text fields: label fields clearly, use hint/placeholder text with examples ("name@example.com"); show errors next to the field with instructive phrasing ("Use only letters for your name", not "Don't use numbers or symbols"; never "Invalid name").

## Branding
Apps and games express their unique brand identity in ways that make them instantly recognizable while feeling at home on the platform and giving people a consistent experience.

Beyond the app icon and in-app experience, you can highlight your brand in the App Store (see App Store Marketing Guidelines).

### Best practices
- Use your brand's unique voice and tone in all written communication (e.g., encouragement and optimism via plain words, occasional exclamation marks and emoji, simple sentences).
- Apply your app's accent color judiciously: minimize use on controls; use it intentionally for primary actions or status indicators (unread badges, selected tab icons); move brand color into the content layer, where it scrolls beneath Liquid Glass controls and gets picked up dynamically.
- Consider a custom font: ensure legibility at all sizes and support for bold text and Dynamic Type; custom font for headlines/subheadings with system fonts for body and captions works well.
- Express your brand with familiar components: customize appearance only while preserving familiar sizing, placement, and behavior appropriate to the platform.
- Ensure branding always defers to content: avoid screen space devoted to pure brand assets.
- Use standard patterns consistently so even a highly stylized interface stays approachable: expected UI locations, standard symbols for common actions, established navigation and modality conventions.
- Resist displaying your logo throughout the app unless it provides context — people seldom need reminding which app they're using.
- Avoid using the launch screen as a branding opportunity (it disappears too quickly to convey anything); consider a welcome or onboarding screen instead.
- Follow Apple's trademark guidelines: Apple trademarks must not appear in your app name or images.
