## Design principles
Fundamental principles that guide design across Apple platforms, based on a deep understanding of how people think, feel, and interact with the world — they are tools for weighing competing priorities and making key design decisions.

### Best practices

**Purpose — make something meaningful**
- Create value: at every stage ask what the product is for and whether the design serves that purpose.
- Keep focused: prioritize the features people need most by aligning with how they want to use the app, and make those features truly great.
- Find new ways to solve the problem: investigate existing solutions instead of re-creating them; define what sets the product apart and reflect it in the design.

**Agency — let people do things their own way**
- Stay out of the way: get people directly to the task or content at hand; the best designs are unobtrusive and present when needed.
- Give people the freedom to explore: don't lock them into specific flows or modes; when a guided flow is necessary, make it easy to skip or escape.
- Help people recover from mistakes: build forgiveness into the design — recovering from the unexpected shouldn't cost people their time or work.

**Responsibility — act in people's best interest**
- Be fully transparent about what the product does and why: make intentions clear from the start; provide a clear rationale when asking for permission, and be clear about what data you collect and how you use it.
- Keep people's information safe: collect only what the product needs, handle it with care, and anticipate and prevent ways it could be misused or cause harm.

**Familiarity — build on what people know**
- Use concepts that people already understand from the real world and other software.
- Keep visuals and interactions consistent: once you establish a behavior or appearance, apply it throughout the design.
- Provide clear feedback: show when controls are available, indicate when content changes, and use system patterns to display alerts and offer choices.

**Flexibility — adapt to diverse contexts and needs**
- Design for everyone: treat accessibility as a priority from the start and design inclusively to reach the broadest audience.
- Preserve a person's context: keep content and controls in consistent, predictable positions as the design adapts across platforms and configurations; use natural animations to ease transitions.
- Consider a variety of input methods — voice, touch, keyboard, and more — so more people can use the product the way that works best for them.
- Approach every platform with intention, giving each platform you support the same level of care.

**Simplicity — be clear and direct**
- Include just what's necessary: simplicity isn't minimalism; keep the important things close by and let the others fall away.
- Be concise: choose exactly the words you need to convey a concept or label a control.
- Establish hierarchy: prioritize recognizable controls and a consistent structure that helps people understand where they are and what comes next.

**Craft — care about every detail**
- Quality sets the tone: be deliberate with each decision — stunning visuals, smooth animations, precise wording, thoughtful audio.
- Experiment and iterate: prototype early, be willing to discard what doesn't work, set a high bar for every feature, and test in real-world settings.
- Maintain your craft: keep the interface current with the latest platform capabilities and design patterns; design is an ongoing commitment.

**Delight — make it human**
- Identify the emotion you want to inspire (a fitness app might energize, a meditation app might calm) and let it shape the design.
- Create defining moments: from a button press to an error message, consider whether each moment can reflect the spirit of your design.
- Don't mistake delight for decoration: never let the pursuit of delight get in the way of the product's core purpose.
- Consider the whole: delight is the sum of the freedom to act, the safety to explore, familiar metaphors, and flexible transitions between contexts.
